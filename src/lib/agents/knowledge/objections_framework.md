# Objection Handling Framework (Never Split the Difference & Influence)

This rulebook trains the **Community Agent** (to extract and categorize objections) and the **Script Writer** (to resolve objections inside video scripts).

---

## 1. Negotiation Tactics (From *Never Split the Difference*)

When students express friction (e.g., "Too expensive", "No laptop", "Scared to fail"), do not argue. Use the following tactical empathy mechanics:

### A. Labeling the Fear
Isolate and state the unsaid emotion. Start with:
- *"It seems like you are worried about making a financial commitment right now."*
- *"It looks like you feel intimidated by the math or coding requirements."*
- *"It sounds like you've tried other online tutorials and felt abandoned."*

### B. Calibrated Questions
Use "How" and "What" questions to force the prospect to think, rather than letting them default to defensive "No" responses:
- *"What is holding you back from investing in your coding skills today?"*
- *"How will your career trajectory change if you don't acquire this skill in the next 12 months?"*
- *"What happens if you run into a bug and don't have a mentor to guide you?"*

### C. Accusation Audit
Proactively state their doubts before they can speak them. This disarms resistance:
- *"You probably think this is just another expensive, self-paced course where you'll get stuck on day two and quit."*
- *"You might think that because you don't own a high-end laptop, you can't participate in Sena Academy."*

---

## 2. Persuasion Triggers (From *Influence*)

Integrate these triggers to handle objections and build trust:

### A. Social Proof
- Address objections by telling a story of a student who had the exact same issue and succeeded.
- *Example*: *"Michael didn't have a laptop either; he used his phone to study documentation and coded on a borrowed computer at the local library. Now he's building full-stack apps."*

### B. Liking & Authority
- Position Sena Academy instructors not as remote professors, but as peers who have been in their exact position.
- Emphasize structured mentor support, which sets this apart from basic YouTube tutorials.

### C. Commitment & Consistency
- Connect coding to their existing identity.
- Ask: *"Do you see yourself as someone who just watches technology happen, or as someone who builds it?"*

## 3. Sena Academy Objection Mapping Rules

| Objection Category | Root Fear | Accusation Audit / Label to Use | Resolution Frame |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Price (Too Expensive)** | Risk of losing money, value uncertainty. | *"It seems like you are worried about the return on your investment."* | Contrast the price against the cost of a university degree or months of wasted self-study. |
| **No Laptop** | Feeling excluded or inadequate. | *"It looks like you feel you can't start because you don't have the perfect setup."* | Emphasize mobile-friendly reading materials, cloud environments (like Replit/GitHub Codespaces), and offline resources. |
| **Fear of Coding (Too Hard)** | Imposter syndrome, fear of failing. | *"It sounds like you think coding is only for math geniuses."* | Detail our step-by-step curriculum, community debugging, and the "build in public" philosophy. |
| **No Time** | Overwhelmed with work/school/exams. | *"It seems like you feel you don't have enough hours in the day to commit."* | Shift focus to micro-learning (15-30 mins/day) and async project milestones. |
